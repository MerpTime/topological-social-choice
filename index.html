Averaging Angles

Each voter’s preference is now a point on the circle \(S^1\). A social choice rule takes everyone’s points and returns a single point for the group. With two voters, that is a function \(\varphi : S^1 \times S^1 \to S^1\). There are three properties that are essential for a fair voting system:

Unanimity

If everyone agrees, the group chooses what they agree on.

\(\varphi(x, x) = x\)

Anonymity

It doesn’t matter who cast which vote.

\(\varphi(x, y) = \varphi(y, x)\)

Continuity

A small change in anyone’s vote causes only a small change in the outcome.

\(\varphi\) is continuous

An intuitive voting system is to average the two angles:
